## 3. Shot list

Five sections, matching the five audio segments. Shoot 16:9, 1920×1080 minimum,
25 or 30 fps, locked frame rate throughout.

### Segment 1 — "Before the city wakes" (0:00–0:22 EN / 0:00–0:27 AR)

2. Empty marble lobby, lights off, slow push in. **4 s**
3. Service lift doors open, a uniformed team steps out, backs to camera. **4 s**
4. Close: gloved hands lifting a mop / opening a tool bag. **3 s**
5. Wide: cleaning crew fanning out across a dark corridor. **4 s**
6. First light hitting a glass facade. **3 s**

10. Maintenance technician opening an electrical panel, torch on. **4 s**
11. Close: multimeter probing a terminal, reading visible. **3 s**
12. Warehouse: forklift moving a pallet, operator in uniform. **4 s**

### Segment 3 — "Fully compliant" (0:48–1:11 EN / 1:01–1:27 AR)

## 4. Casting and wardrobe

Follows the uniform standard already used across the website imagery.

- **Men only** in worker roles, per brand direction.
- Mixed nationalities reflecting the real workforce: Saudi, Indian,
  Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Filipino.
- Ages 25–50. Real workers preferred over models — the brief asks for
  authenticity, and it shows on camera.
- **Uniform:** black long-sleeve polo, gold logo embroidered left chest.
  Black cap with the gold logo centred on the front panel. Black trousers
  and shoes. Rear-facing workers carry a larger emblem on the upper back.
- Supervisors may wear a black soft-shell jacket over the polo.
- Clean, pressed uniforms. No visible third-party branding anywhere in frame.

---

## 5. Grade and look

Match the website so the film and the site feel like one brand.

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| Brand gold | `#b58e4a` |
| Deep brown (backgrounds, titles) | `#241c0f` |
| Cream (text on dark) | `#f8f4ed` |
| Accent used on site | `#8b6c38` |

- Warm highlights, slightly lifted shadows with a brown tint. Avoid a cold
  or teal-and-orange grade — it fights the brand palette.
- Golden hour for exteriors wherever possible.
- Interiors: practical lighting, keep it warm, avoid green fluorescent cast.
- No heavy vignettes, no borders. Clean depth using light and shadow, the same
  design language as the website.

## 7. Music

- Restrained, percussive, building. No vocals — they fight the narration.
- Duck the music by **-12 dB** under every narration segment.
- Let the music breathe alone in the gaps between segments and in the
  final 4 seconds over the end frame.
- Licence it properly. Do not use a track lifted from social media.

---

## 8. Assembling the edit

1. Import all five narration files for one language onto an audio track,
   butted end to end in order, starting at 0:00. That is your spine.
2. Lay the shot list against it, matching **scene changes to segment
   boundaries** listed in section 2.
3. Add the end frame as a still for the final 4 seconds, over segment 5's tail.
4. Music underneath, ducked as described above.
5. Export twice: one English cut, one Arabic cut.

**Export settings**

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Container | MP4 (H.264 + AAC) |
| Resolution | 1920×1080 |
| Frame rate | Same as source, locked |
| Video bitrate | 8–10 Mbps for the master |
| Audio | AAC 192 kbps, 48 kHz, stereo |
| Loudness | Normalise narration to about **-16 LUFS** |

**Web version:** re-encode the master down to roughly **4–6 MB** before putting
it on the site (about 1.5 Mbps, still 1080p). A 25 MB file on the homepage
doubles the page weight and will hurt your load time badly.

## 10. Honest note on AI-generated video

The original brief asked for the video to be regenerated with AI as realistic
Saudi workers. That was not possible here, and the reason is worth recording so
nobody retries it and loses time:

- No video generation model is available in this environment, and no video
  tooling either (no ffmpeg, no moviepy, no OpenCV). Installing ffmpeg was
  attempted and the package is unavailable.
- The brief itself asks for **"real people, real voices — not AI avatars."**
  AI-generated humans would contradict that requirement.
- Current AI video models still struggle with the exact things this film needs:
  consistent faces across shots, correct logo placement on a uniform, legible
  Arabic text, and hands doing detailed tool work. Results tend to look
  convincing for three seconds and wrong for the rest.

What was produced instead is everything that genuinely could be: the full
narration in both languages with a single consistent voice, the branded end
frames, and this production pack. A local Riyadh crew can shoot section 3
in one or two days, and the audio already exists to cut against.
